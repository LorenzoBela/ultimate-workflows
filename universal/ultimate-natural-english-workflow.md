# Natural English with meaning preserved

Use for requested prose drafting, rewriting or tone adjustment. Choose audience and tone from the user and artifact. Clear everyday language is a preference; it does not override factual accuracy, quotations, legal/scientific nuance or requested style.

## Inputs and protected content

Identify intended reader, purpose, format, tone and text to revise. Preserve names, numbers, dates, units, URLs, citations, API identifiers, code, direct quotations, requirements and permission boundaries. If a factual claim looks unsupported, flag it or preserve its uncertainty; do not invent evidence while polishing.

Calibrated uncertainty is substantive. “May fail under load” must not become “will fail under load” without evidence. “We plan to test” must not become “tests pass.” Keep estimates, conditions, limitations and attribution intact.

## Editing process

1. Lead with the useful point. Remove redundant throat-clearing and repetition when they add no meaning.
2. Prefer concrete actors/actions and familiar words when equally precise. Technical terms, passive voice and formal phrasing remain valid when context requires them.
3. Replace vague praise with a concrete supported benefit or remove it. Do not add metrics, certainty, emotional intent or testimonials.
4. Vary sentence length naturally, preserving logical connections. Do not force contractions, fragment sentences or mechanical comma limits into every document.
5. Match voice: direct technical notes, personable team communication, concise executive explanation or the user's requested alternative. Respect multilingual text and source voice.
6. Compare original and revision for semantic drift: same claims, scope, negation, probability, conditions, dates, numbers and cited evidence. Recheck protected content verbatim when it must remain exact.

Words such as “robust,” “facilitate” or “moreover” are not inherently incorrect. Replace contextual filler rather than blindly deleting a blacklist. Do not rewrite quoted language or an identifier just because it appears on a style list.

## Examples

- Before: “We will conduct an investigation of the timeout.”
  After: “We'll investigate the timeout.”
- Before: “The worker may stall if the lease expires during processing.”
  After: “The worker may stall if its lease expires while it processes a job.”
  The uncertainty and condition stay intact.
- Before: “Testing is planned; performance gains are unknown.”
  After: “We plan to test it. We don't yet know whether performance improves.”
  A rewrite does not turn a plan into a result.

## Completion

Deliver usable text in the requested format. Mention material ambiguity outside the artifact if it affects meaning. For file edits preserve unrelated content and inspect the diff; for a high-consequence document have an appropriate reviewer verify substantive meaning. No claim of human authorship or guaranteed AI-detection evasion is implied.
